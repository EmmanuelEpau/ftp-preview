The Dating Project: questions people ask

The Dating Project is a documentary film from Family Theater Productions, not a dating app. This is its official page: familytheater.org/thedatingproject.

About the film

What is The Dating Project?

The Dating Project is a 2018 documentary from Family Theater Productions about why dating has nearly disappeared among young adults, and how to bring it back. It follows five single people between 18 and 40 as they try to date in the age of social media, apps and hookup culture, with Boston College professor Kerry Cronin as their guide.

It was directed by Jonathan Cipiti and made by Family Theater Productions with Paulist Productions and Mpower Pictures.

Is The Dating Project a dating app or a dating service?

No. The Dating Project is a documentary film, not an app, a matchmaking service or a membership. It has no connection to the Christian dating app called The Dating Project at thedatingproject.co, which is a separate company.

The film's official page is familytheater.org/thedatingproject. The old film address, thedatingprojectmovie.com, leads here.

Who made The Dating Project?

Jonathan Cipiti directed the film. Megan Harrington, Catherine Fowler Sample and John Shepherd produced it, and Harrington and Fowler Sample wrote it. The studios behind it are Family Theater Productions, the Hollywood film and TV studio founded in 1947 by Venerable Patrick Peyton, Paulist Productions and Mpower Pictures (full credits).

When did The Dating Project come out?

The film was first shown at the USA Film Festival in Dallas on 27 April 2017. It opened in cinemas for one night on 17 April 2018 through Fathom Events, in more than 600 theaters, with a filmed conversation with Kerry Cronin. The DVD followed in June 2018, and since 29 September 2026 the full film has streamed free on EWTN+.

How long is The Dating Project, and what is it rated?

The Dating Project runs about 70 minutes (EWTN+ lists 69). It has no MPA rating: the DVD is listed as not rated, and EWTN+ shows TV-MA. The film talks frankly about hookup culture, and reviewers have recommended it for discussion groups from high school age to adults.

The people in it

Who is in The Dating Project?

Five single people: Matt and Shanzi, students at Boston College; Cecilia, in her twenties in Chicago; Rasheeda, in her thirties in New York; and Chris, in his forties in Los Angeles. Boston College professor Kerry Cronin, who gives her students the dating assignment, guides the film.

Who is Kerry Cronin?

Kerry Cronin is Associate Professor of the Practice and Director of the Perspectives Program in the philosophy department at Boston College. On campus she is known as the Date Doctor. She began giving her dating assignment after learning that only one of eight graduating seniors she spoke to had been on a date in college.

What happened to Chris from The Dating Project?

Chris Meehan is now a marriage and family therapist. He was the guest on episode 2 of Intentional Dating, the Family Theater Productions podcast that carries on the conversation the film started.

The Dating Assignment

What is the Dating Assignment?

The Dating Assignment is the exercise at the heart of The Dating Project: Kerry Cronin asks her students to go on a simple first date, in person, with clear rules that take the pressure off. At Boston College it is known as Cronin's date assignment. The film follows the five singles as they try it.

What are the rules of the Dating Assignment?
  • Ask in person, and go on the date within three days.
  • Ask someone new you are romantically interested in, and say plainly that it is a date.
  • Keep it to 45 to 90 minutes, and have a plan.
  • If you ask, you pay, and spend no more than $20.
  • No physical contact beyond an “A-frame” hug.
  • Tell three people you are going, and go alone.

The full sheet is free to download and share (PDF).

Why did people stop dating?

The Dating Project looks at how hookup culture, social media and dating apps changed the way young adults meet, and why the simple first date became rare. Kerry Cronin's answer is to bring it back on purpose, one small, low-pressure date at a time.

Faith and who it is for

Is The Dating Project for Christians? Is it a Catholic film?

The Dating Project was made by two Catholic studios, Family Theater Productions and Paulist Productions, with Mpower Pictures. Faith is part of the story but not its center: Kerry Cronin calls the religious content “a light touch,” and Rasheeda is the single who speaks most openly about her faith. The film is for anyone who wants to date with more intention, whatever their background.

Is The Dating Project appropriate for teenagers?

Reviewers have recommended it for discussion groups from high school age to adults, and Family Theater Productions publishes a curriculum written for high school students. Because the film talks openly about hookup culture, parents and leaders may want to watch it first.

Is there a study guide or curriculum for The Dating Project?

Yes. Family Theater Productions offers free curriculum guides in five versions: young adults, high school, college, families, and fathers and sons. Ascension Press also publishes A Guide to The Dating Project by Jason and Crystalina Evert.

How can my parish, school or group show The Dating Project?

Write to license@hcfm.org with the name of your group, the date you have in mind and roughly how many people will come. The team will set up the screening with you.

Related series

What is Dating Decoded?

Dating Decoded: Beyond The Dating Project is a free YouTube series from Family Theater Productions, made from footage shot for the film that was never seen. It launched on 29 September 2026, the day the film arrived on EWTN+, with episodes such as “What Is Dating?” and “Hookup Culture Has Replaced Dating, Here's Why.”

What is Intentional Dating?

Intentional Dating: Relationship Goals for Christians is a Family Theater Productions podcast that picks up ideas from The Dating Project. Nick Vicinanzo and matchmaker Aly Conti host it, with guests including Chris Meehan from the film, Jason Evert and Tim O'Malley. Its ten episodes, launched 12 March 2026, are on YouTube, Apple Podcasts, Spotify and Amazon Music.
